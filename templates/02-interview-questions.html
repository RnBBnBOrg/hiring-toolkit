Interview Question Bank

Organized by competency so you can mix and match based on the role. Pick 8-12 questions per interview — don't try to ask everything.

Core Competencies (Ask Everyone)

Work Ethic & Reliability

  1. Tell me about a time you had to juggle multiple deadlines. How did you decide what to prioritize?
  2. Describe a situation where you had to do something you'd never done before at work. How did you figure it out?
  3. What does "doing a good job" look like to you? How do you know when you've hit that standard?

Communication

  1. Tell me about a time you had to explain something complex to someone who wasn't familiar with the topic.
  2. Describe a miscommunication at work. What happened and how did you resolve it?
  3. How do you prefer to receive feedback? Can you give me an example of feedback that changed how you work?

Problem-Solving

  1. Walk me through the last time something went wrong at work that you had to fix. What did you do?
  2. Tell me about a time you identified a problem before anyone else noticed. What did you do about it?
  3. Describe a situation where you had to make a decision without all the information you wanted.

Culture & Fit

  1. What kind of work environment brings out your best work?
  2. Tell me about the best team you've been part of. What made it work?
  3. What's something you're genuinely interested in learning right now — work-related or not?

Role-Specific: Operations & Admin

  1. Describe a process you built or improved. What was it before, what did you change, and what was the result?
  2. How do you keep track of everything when you're managing multiple projects for different people?
  3. Tell me about a time you had to enforce a policy or process that someone pushed back on.
  4. What tools do you use to stay organized? Walk me through your system.

Role-Specific: Customer-Facing / Sales

  1. Tell me about a client or customer relationship you're most proud of. What made it successful?
  2. Describe a time a client was unhappy. How did you handle it and what was the outcome?
  3. How do you balance being responsive to clients with protecting your own time and boundaries?
  4. Walk me through how you'd onboard a new client in your first week here. What would you want to know?

Role-Specific: Technical / Specialist

  1. Walk me through a project you've completed recently. What was the challenge, what decisions did you make, and what would you do differently?
  2. How do you stay current in your field? What's something you've learned recently that changed how you work?
  3. Describe a time you had to push back on a request because it wasn't technically feasible. How did you handle it?
  4. Tell me about a time you had to work on something with ambiguous or incomplete requirements.

Role-Specific: Management / Leadership

  1. How do you handle a team member who's underperforming? Walk me through your approach.
  2. Tell me about a difficult decision you made that affected your team. How did you communicate it?
  3. How do you balance getting work done yourself with developing the people on your team?
  4. Describe a time you inherited a team or process that wasn't working. What did you do?

Role-Specific: Entry-Level / Junior

  1. What's something you taught yourself? How did you go about learning it?
  2. Tell me about a time you received critical feedback. How did you respond?
  3. What interests you about this type of work? What do you want to learn in this role?
  4. Describe a group project (school, volunteer, work) where things didn't go as planned. What was your role in getting it back on track?

Red Flag Questions

These aren't questions you ask — they're signals to watch for in their answers:

SignalWhat to Watch For
Blame patternEvery story involves someone else being the problem. Never takes ownership.
Vague answersCan't give specific examples. "I'm a hard worker" without evidence.
BadmouthingSpeaks negatively about every past employer, manager, or coworker.
No questions for youShows no curiosity about the role, team, or company.
Compensation-only focusOnly asks about pay, perks, and time off — never about the work itself.
Can't explain gapsWork history gaps aren't a red flag, but being evasive about them is.

Tips

  • Behavioral questions beat hypothetical ones. "Tell me about a time you..." reveals what they actually do. "What would you do if..." reveals what they think sounds good.
  • Ask the same core questions to every candidate for the same role. Consistency is how you compare fairly.
  • Listen for specifics. Good candidates give you details — names, numbers, timelines, outcomes. Vague candidates give you generalities.
  • Leave time for their questions. A candidate's questions tell you as much as their answers. Budget 10-15 minutes at the end.
  • Take notes during the interview. Don't rely on memory — use the scorecard (next template) to capture your assessment while it's fresh.

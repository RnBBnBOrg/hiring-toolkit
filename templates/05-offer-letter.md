# Offer Letter Templates

Professional, clear offer letters that set the right tone and cover the details candidates care about.

---

## Template 1: Standard Full-Time Offer

---

**[Company Name]**
[Company Address]
[Date]

Dear [Candidate Name],

We're excited to extend an offer for you to join [Company Name] as [Job Title]. After meeting with you, we're confident you'll be a great addition to our team, and we'd love to move forward.

**The details:**

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Position** | [Job Title] |
| **Reports to** | [Manager Name, Title] |
| **Start date** | [Date] |
| **Location** | [On-site / Remote / Hybrid — details] |
| **Employment type** | Full-time, [exempt/non-exempt] |
| **Compensation** | $[Amount] per [year/hour] |
| **Pay schedule** | [Bi-weekly / Semi-monthly / Monthly] |

**Benefits:**

- [Health insurance — coverage details, employer contribution, effective date]
- [PTO — X days/year, accrual details]
- [Retirement — 401(k) match details, eligibility timeline]
- [Other — dental, vision, life insurance, disability]
- [Perks — remote stipend, equipment, professional development budget]

**Conditions of employment:**

This offer is contingent upon:
- [Successful completion of a background check]
- [Proof of eligibility to work in the United States (I-9)]
- [Signing of confidentiality / non-disclosure agreement]
- [Any other conditions]

This is an at-will employment offer, meaning either you or [Company Name] may end the employment relationship at any time, with or without cause or notice.

**To accept:**

Please sign and return this letter by [deadline — typically 5-7 business days]. If you have questions or would like to discuss any part of this offer, please reach out to me directly at [phone/email].

We're genuinely looking forward to working with you.

Sincerely,

[Hiring Manager Name]
[Title]
[Company Name]

---

**Acceptance:**

I accept this offer of employment under the terms described above.

Signature: _________________________ Date: _____________

Print name: _________________________

## Template 3: Contract / 1099 Offer

---

**[Company Name]**
[Date]

Dear [Contractor Name],

We'd like to engage you as an independent contractor for [Company Name] for the following scope of work. This letter outlines the key terms — a formal independent contractor agreement will follow for your review and signature.

**Engagement details:**

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| **Role** | [Title / Description] |
| **Scope** | [Brief description of deliverables or responsibilities] |
| **Duration** | [Start date] through [End date or "ongoing until terminated by either party with X days notice"] |
| **Rate** | $[Amount] per [hour / project / month] |
| **Payment terms** | [Net 15 / Net 30 / Upon invoice submission] |
| **Estimated hours** | [X hours/week or X hours total, if applicable] |

**Important notes:**

- As an independent contractor, you are responsible for your own taxes, insurance, and benefits. [Company Name] will not withhold taxes or provide employee benefits.
- You will receive a 1099-NEC for tax reporting purposes if payments exceed $600 in a calendar year.
- [Intellectual property / work product ownership terms]
- [Non-disclosure / confidentiality requirements]

Either party may terminate this engagement with [X days] written notice.

If this aligns with your expectations, please confirm by [deadline]. I'll follow up with the full contractor agreement for signature.

Looking forward to working together.

Sincerely,

[Hiring Manager Name]
[Title]

## Tips

- **Get these reviewed by a lawyer for your state.** Employment law varies by location. These templates give you the structure, but your attorney should sign off on the legal language before you send them.
- **Be specific about compensation.** Vague language causes confusion. State the exact amount, the exact pay period, and when the first check arrives.
- **Give them time but not too much time.** 5-7 business days is standard. Less feels pressured, more lets counter-offers creep in.
- **Keep the tone warm.** This is your first official communication as their employer. "We're excited" is better than "Please find enclosed."
- **Don't bury the salary.** Candidates scroll straight to compensation. Put it near the top where it's easy to find.
- **Follow up the next day.** After sending the offer, call or email to ask if they have questions. Silence after an offer feels cold.

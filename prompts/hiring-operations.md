# AI Prompts: Hiring Operations

Prompts for the logistics of hiring — offer letters, rejection messages, pipeline management, and process improvement.

---

## Prompt 1: Draft an Offer Letter

> I need to write an offer letter for a new hire. Here are the details:
>
> - **Candidate name:** [Name]
> - **Position:** [Job Title]
> - **Employment type:** [Full-time / Part-time / Contract / 1099]
> - **Start date:** [Date]
> - **Compensation:** [$X per year/hour]
> - **Benefits:** [List what you offer]
> - **Reports to:** [Manager name and title]
> - **Location:** [On-site / Remote / Hybrid]
> - **Contingencies:** [Background check, drug test, I-9, NDA, etc.]
> - **Acceptance deadline:** [X business days]
>
> Write a professional but warm offer letter. Include all the details clearly — don't make them dig for the salary. Add an acceptance signature block at the bottom.
>
> Important: Flag anything I should run by a lawyer before sending.

## Prompt 2: Write a Rejection Email

> I need to send a rejection to a candidate. Here's the context:
>
> - **Candidate name:** [Name]
> - **Role:** [Job Title]
> - **Stage they reached:** [Application only / Phone screen / Full interview / Final round]
> - **Keep warm for future?** [Yes / No]
> - **Anything specific to acknowledge?** [Optional — e.g., "They had a particularly strong technical interview"]
>
> Write a rejection email that's respectful and professional. Don't give specific reasons for the rejection (legal exposure), but don't be generic either. Match the depth to how far they got in the process — a post-interview rejection should be more personal than a post-application one.

## Prompt 3: Counter-Offer Response Strategy

> A candidate I've extended an offer to says they have a counter-offer from their current employer [or another company]. Here's the situation:
>
> - **Our offer:** [$X, Job Title]
> - **Their counter:** [$Y from Current Employer / Competing Company]
> - **What we can flex on:** [Salary / Title / Remote days / Start date / Signing bonus / PTO / Nothing]
> - **How badly we want this person (1-10):** [X]
> - **What our backup plan is:** [Strong backup candidate / Weak pipeline / Would need to restart search]
>
> Help me decide:
> 1. Should I match, counter, or hold firm?
> 2. If I counter, what should I offer and how should I frame it?
> 3. Give me the exact email or talking points for the conversation
> 4. What questions should I ask them to figure out if this is really about money or something else?

## Prompt 4: Build a Hiring Process from Scratch

> I'm hiring for the first time and don't have a formal process. Help me build one.
>
> **Context:**
> - **Company size:** [X people]
> - **Role I'm hiring for:** [Job Title]
> - **Budget for recruiting:** [Approximate budget or "minimal"]
> - **Who's involved in hiring decisions:** [Just me / Me + team lead / Panel]
> - **Timeline:** [Need someone by X date]
>
> Create a step-by-step hiring process including:
> 1. Where to post the job (free and paid options for my budget)
> 2. How to screen resumes efficiently
> 3. Phone screen structure (what to ask, how long)
> 4. Interview structure (questions, who should be in the room)
> 5. Evaluation method (how to compare candidates fairly)
> 6. Offer and onboarding steps
> 7. Timeline — how long each step should take
>
> Keep it simple. I don't need an enterprise HR process — I need something that works for a small team.

## Prompt 5: Audit My Hiring Process

> Here's how we currently hire: [describe your process step by step]
>
> Audit it for:
> - **Speed:** Where are we losing time? Where do candidates drop off?
> - **Fairness:** Are we evaluating consistently or relying on gut feel?
> - **Candidate experience:** Would a strong candidate enjoy this process or dread it?
> - **Legal risk:** Anything we're doing that could create liability?
> - **Missing steps:** What are we skipping that we shouldn't be?
>
> Give me a prioritized list of improvements — quick wins first, then structural changes.

## Prompt 6: Write a New Hire Welcome Message

> I have a new hire starting soon. Help me write a welcome message.
>
> - **New hire name:** [Name]
> - **Role:** [Job Title]
> - **Start date:** [Date]
> - **First day details:** [What time to arrive, where to go, what to bring, who they'll meet]
> - **Tone:** [Casual / Professional / Warm]
>
> Write two versions:
> 1. **Email to the new hire** — What they need to know before Day 1 (logistics, what to expect, any pre-work)
> 2. **Message to the team** — Announcing the new hire (their background, what they'll be working on, how to welcome them)

## Prompt 7: Compensation Research

> I'm hiring a [Job Title] and need to set a competitive salary range.
>
> **Context:**
> - **Location:** [City, State / Remote]
> - **Company size:** [X employees]
> - **Industry:** [Your industry]
> - **Experience level needed:** [Junior / Mid / Senior]
> - **What I was planning to offer:** [$X - $Y]
>
> Help me:
> 1. Is my planned range competitive for this market?
> 2. What factors should push me higher or lower?
> 3. What non-salary components (benefits, equity, flexibility) can make a lower salary competitive?
> 4. Where should I look for up-to-date salary data for this role?
>
> Note: I know you don't have real-time salary data. Give me frameworks and point me to reliable sources.

## Prompt 8: Create an Employee Referral Program

> I want to start an employee referral program. My team has [X] people and I typically hire [Y] people per year.
>
> Design a referral program that:
> - Incentivizes quality referrals without creating awkwardness
> - Has clear rules (who's eligible, when the bonus pays out, what counts as a referral)
> - Is simple enough to manage without HR software
> - Includes the announcement message I'd send to the team
>
> What referral bonus amount makes sense for a company my size?
